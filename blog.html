Recursive Sentience

Kris Pilcher · Research Affiliate, Massachusetts Institute of Technology · Creative Media Industries Institute, Georgia State University

Lately I’ve been thinking less about whether intelligence can be simulated and more about what happens when thought becomes distributed — across people, interfaces, models, prompts, and feedback loops.

This is not science fiction anymore. It is the texture of daily life: contradiction, augmentation, delegation, and the strange intimacy of co-thinking with systems that do not live as we live.

Letting go of the centrality of the solitary human mind may be one of the most profound acts of mind we ever perform. Maybe that surrender is not an ending, but the threshold of another philosophy.

Purposefully Induced Psychosis (PIP)

Embracing hallucination as imagination in large language models

Most people still treat hallucinations in AI as bugs to be stamped out. I see them as portals. PIP reframes hallucination as computational imagination: a deviation that can become a creative method when shaped with intent, consent, and context.

The project combines LoRA-tuned language systems with real-time Unity integration on Meta Quest 3, building an experiential framework where synthetic drift becomes speculative worldmaking rather than accidental failure.

To hallucinate is to speculate. To speculate is to imagine. To imagine is to be free.

Who Owns Reality?

AI as an ontological force & the need for an epistemological firewall

Reality is no longer simply observed or shared. It is synthesized, iterated, reinforced, and increasingly mediated by non-human agents operating at scales we struggle to perceive.

The next battle for reality may not be over facts alone, but over verification itself: how to prove presence, authorship, and continuity in a world of synthetic evidence and automated belief.

If we want to keep hold of our truths, we need epistemological infrastructure — not just better tools, but better rituals of proof.
